## Findings

### [High] Disposing the Gemini scene leaves its animation loop and scene resources alive

- **File:** `src/gemini3.1-pro/features/cubes-exploding/CubesExplodingScene.js:256`
- **Problem:** `animate()` schedules the next animation frame without retaining its identifier. `dispose()` removes two event listeners and disposes the renderer, but never cancels the pending frame, clears `renderer`, or disposes the sphere, cubes, explosions, their geometries, or their materials.
- **Evidence:** After `dispose()` at line 282, the queued callback reaches the `renderer.render(...)` call at line 271 because `this.renderer` remains non-null. Each collision also creates an `Explosion` whose meshes are removed but whose shared geometry and material are never disposed.
- **Impact:** Navigating away from or replacing the feature leaves a live render/update loop and GPU resources behind. Re-entering the feature can progressively consume CPU, memory, and WebGL resources.
- **Recommendation:** Store the request-animation-frame identifier, cancel it during disposal, dispose every owned mesh/material/geometry and active explosion, and clear references so a queued callback becomes a no-op.

### [High] Gemini's sphere containment ignores the actual cube extent

- **File:** `src/gemini3.1-pro/features/cubes-exploding/CubesExplodingScene.js:170`
- **Problem:** Cubes have a random edge length from 1 to 5, but spawning always scales an intersection point by `0.8` and boundary detection uses the fixed margin `sphereRadius - 1`.
- **Evidence:** At the default sphere radius of 8, a cube with edge length 5 has a bounding-sphere radius of about 4.33. A sphere-surface pick becomes a center point at radius 6.4 after the `0.8` multiplier, leaving the cube's outer extent at about 10.73, outside the radius-8 boundary before simulation starts. The fixed one-unit bounce margin at line 213 cannot correct this.
- **Impact:** Large cubes can visibly start or remain outside the transparent boundary, contradicting the feature's containment and bounce behavior.
- **Recommendation:** Calculate the cube half-extent from its scale, clamp spawn positions to `sphereRadius - halfExtent`, and use that same extent for boundary response and repositioning after an overshoot.

### [Medium] Coordinate-system scaling discards the cube's requested base size

- **Files:** `src/gpt5.3-codex/features/CoordinateSystem.js:101`; `src/gemini3.1-pro/features/coordinate-system/CoordinateSystem.js:101`
- **Problem:** `drawCube(startX, startY, size)` saves `_cubeProps.size` as `1`, regardless of the supplied `size`. Therefore, `scaleCube(multiplier)` is always relative to 1 instead of the cube it last drew.
- **Evidence:** A direct reproduction calling `drawCube(0, 0, 2)` followed by `scaleCube(2)` drew a right edge at x=275 rather than the expected x=294 for a four-unit cube at the default origin and scale.
- **Impact:** Callers that use the public scaling API receive the wrong cube dimensions, and repeated scaling cannot preserve or compound the original size as the method's comments promise.
- **Recommendation:** Persist the received `size` in `_cubeProps` and define scaling consistently as either relative to that base size or relative to the current size. Add direct unit coverage for non-default and repeated scales.

### [Medium] Grok's UI allows a cube that cannot fit inside the selected sphere

- **Files:** `src/grok4.5/features/cubes-exploding/renderCubesExplodingSection.js:51`; `src/grok4.5/features/cubes-exploding/BabylonCubesExplodingController.js:144`
- **Problem:** The UI allows a sphere scale of 4 while cube edge length can be 5. Such a cube has a half-space diagonal of about 4.33, which is larger than the sphere radius.
- **Evidence:** `clampPointInsideSphere()` clamps the center to 0.01 in this case, but the cube's outer extent remains about 4.34, still outside the radius-4 sphere. No spawn-time validation, scale cap, or resize reconciliation prevents this state.
- **Impact:** A valid user configuration can create cubes that intersect the boundary immediately, making the advertised containment behavior impossible.
- **Recommendation:** Constrain maximum cube scale to the active sphere radius, constrain the minimum sphere scale to a viable cube size, or reject/reduce an impossible cube before creating its mesh. Reconcile existing cubes when the sphere is reduced.

### [Medium] Cube motion and explosion duration depend on the browser frame rate

- **Files:** `src/gemini3.1-pro/features/cubes-exploding/CubesExplodingScene.js:28`; `src/gemini3.1-pro/features/cubes-exploding/CubesExplodingScene.js:205`; `src/grok4.5/features/cubes-exploding/BabylonCubesExplodingController.js:335`
- **Problem:** Both implementations add fixed velocity and rotation values once per render frame. The Gemini explosion lifetime is also fixed at 30 frames.
- **Evidence:** At 144 Hz, cubes move, rotate, and Gemini explosions expire roughly 2.4 times faster than at 60 Hz; in a throttled/background tab the same simulation becomes much slower.
- **Impact:** Rendering hardware and browser scheduling change the observable physics and effects, so motion is inconsistent across users and devices.
- **Recommendation:** Pass elapsed time into simulation updates, express velocity/rotation in per-second units, and use a time-based explosion lifetime. Add deterministic tests that compare equal simulated elapsed time across different frame intervals.
